Computational Intelligence, Data and Language Research Lab · Jahangirnagar University

  • Mechanistic Interpretability: conducted BanglaLens, a layer-wise logit lens analysis of Bangla generation failures in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on a linguist-verified dataset of 1,000 concept words across four parts of speech (verified by three native annotators using Fleiss’ κ). BEGQA: Bangla Evidence-Grounded Question Answering for Government Services with Claim-Level Verification and Evidence-Aware Abstention

Under review NSysS 2026, BUET

Citizens need correct information about fees, dates, deadlines, documents and service steps, but a language model can sound fluent while adding a requirement that is not in the official text. BEGQA is a Bangla question-answering system that answers only from official government documents and checks each answer before it is shown.

48official government documents
1,304Bangla evaluation questions
93.3%Evidence Recall@5, up from 90.2% with reranking
84.1%of well-formed answers judged correct
97.9%of citations valid
90.0%abstention recall (36 of 40 out-of-collection questions)
  • Knowledge base: 48 official Bangladeshi documents over eight service domains, including NID, birth and death registration, land, passports, tax and trade licensing, split into 1,063 chunks of verbatim text.
  • Retrieval and reranking: BGE-M3 embeddings in ChromaDB, with a bge-reranker-v2-m3 cross-encoder reranking the candidates. The whole pipeline works on Bangla text with no translation.
  • Citation-forced generation: the answer is generated from the top five passages, with a source citation at the end of every sentence.
  • Claim-level verification: the answer is split into small claims, and each claim is checked against its own cited passage by an entailment check.
  • Numeric Consistency Guard: a separate deterministic rule checks that the numbers in each claim, such as fees and dates, appear in the source. It detected 193 of 200 deliberately changed numbers (96.5%).
  • Evidence-aware decision: from the per-claim results the system returns the full answer, a filtered answer with unsupported claims removed, or an abstention.
  • Genre-aware prompting: a prompt applied only to statutory documents raised their correct-answer rate from 50.8% to 70.6%.
  • Scope: well-formed answers were produced for 82.7% of questions. The safety and fairness tests are small and the study uses a single document collection.

BanglaLens: A Layer-Wise White-Box Analysis of Bangla Generation Failures in Multilingual Large Language Models Using Logit Lens

Submitted Pre-submission Mentorship Program, Student Research Workshop (SRW), EACL 2027

Multilingual language models often produce weaker output in Bangla than in high-resource languages. BanglaLens applies a layer-wise logit lens to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct to find where Bangla generation breaks down.

1,000linguist-verified concept words
84.36%Translation Loss Proportion in Llama
95.4% vs 16.9%Qwen accuracy, Bangla vs English input
  • White-box, layer-wise: the analysis inspects the model's internal computation layer by layer, not just its final outputs, on 1,000 concept words across four parts of speech verified by three native annotators.
  • Attributing failures: English- and Bangla-source conditions are scored with exact match and LaBSE semantic similarity, and Translation Loss Proportion (TLP) attributes each failure to the translation stage or an upstream task failure.
  • Architecture-dependent failures: a translation barrier in Llama and a cross-lingual routing failure in Qwen.
